# Competitor Landscape — IELTS Preparation

## Purpose

Map the learner-facing alternatives that define user expectations. This is a public-source scan, not a claim of product quality or market share.

## Method and decision status

- **Method:** desk research of public offer/pricing pages on 2026-08-06; no account purchase, learner interview, usability test or cohort data.
- **Evidence strength:** useful for observable positioning/features only; insufficient for adoption, outcome, retention or willingness-to-pay claims.
- **Decision status:** supports a *hypothesis* that Writing Task 2 feedback → fix → retest is a differentiated wedge. It does not approve positioning, pricing or launch claim.

## Observed categories

| Category | Public example | Observable offer | Implication for LenBands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official preparation ecosystem | British Council IELTS Ready | Practice tests, guided modules, personalised plan/progress; Premium bundled with booking | LenBands must not compete on "official" status or raw test volume |
| Course + live support | E2 IELTS packages | Lessons, practice, mock tests, AI marking and human/live support across packages | Human support is a different value proposition; LenBands needs transparent automated quality rather than pretending equivalence |
| Premium expert feedback course | IELTS Advantage VIP | Diagnostic, roadmap, structured materials and personal Writing/Speaking feedback | The market values diagnosis and feedback; LenBands wedge must be error → fix → retest proof |

## Design conclusions

1. Official providers own trust, institutional affiliation and large practice libraries.
2. Premium courses monetize human/expert feedback, structured roadmaps and accountability.
3. LenBands should not position as an "official score" substitute or generic content library.
4. The defensible product hypothesis remains: evidence-based feedback, personal Error Graph, review scheduling and retest proof at a cost/latency a human-feedback product cannot match.

## Falsifiable hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Evidence needed | Decision if false |
|---|---|---|
| Learners value retest proof more than a one-off estimated band | 10+ interviews + pilot behavior | Reposition around faster feedback/structured practice |
| Learners will trust automated feedback if evidence/confidence are visible | Feedback helpfulness + disagreement rate | Strengthen benchmark/disclosure or narrow claims |
| Writing Task 2 is a strong entry wedge | Activation → submission → fix → retest funnel | Test another segment/question type |

## Limits

Public pages describe offers, not actual learner outcomes, retention or willingness to pay. Those require interviews and pilot data.
